Roofing website design in Wellington: wind, hills and lighter roofs

Wellington roofs face wind most cities never see, on houses perched on hillsides with awkward access. A roofer's website should show they know how to work in those conditions.

Short answer

A Wellington roofing website should prove you can work in wind and on steep sites. Show photos of hillside jobs and scaffolding, write pages for wind damage, leaks and lighter reroofs, cover the Hutt and Porirua as well as the city, and display your LBP details.

Wellington is often called the windiest city in New Zealand, and its houses sit on some of the steepest suburban land in the country. Roofing here means working at height on hillsides, in weather that changes fast, on roofs hit by northerlies and southerlies alike.

Homeowners know this. When they look for a roofer, they want signs you have done this kind of work before.

01What should a Wellington roofing site show first?

Many roofing websites show flat, sunny photos of finished roofs. In Wellington, the more convincing photos show the conditions: scaffolding on a steep section in Brooklyn, a reroof on a hillside villa in Mt Victoria, a crew working on a house with a cable car or a long set of steps for access. Those images tell a homeowner you can deal with their property.

A short page on how you handle access, wind and weather delays also helps. Many homeowners worry about how long a job will take when the forecast keeps changing. Explaining your approach in advance builds trust.

02Wind, rain and leaks

Wind drives rain sideways, into places it would never reach in calmer cities. Flashings, ridge caps, fixings and spouting all take more stress here. Loose iron and lifted sheets are common call-outs after a big blow.

Spouting deserves its own mention. Hillside sections send a lot of water down the roof in heavy rain, and undersized or blocked spouting overflows fast. A page on spouting replacement, with photos of jobs on steep sites, picks up searches that general leak pages miss.

  • Write a page for wind damage: lifted or loose iron, flapping flashings, damaged ridging.
  • Write a page for leaks, using the words homeowners use: water through the ceiling, damp patches after a southerly.
  • Keep the phone number visible on mobile, and add photo upload to your form.

03Why do Wellington owners switch to lighter roofs?

Wellington sits on active faults, so earthquake damage is a real concern for older homes. Since then, many owners have replaced heavy tile roofs and removed brick chimneys, choosing lightweight long-run steel instead. If you do this work, it is worth a dedicated page that explains the process and shows examples.

04Area pages for the region

The Wellington region is spread across valleys and harbours, and locals know where each suburb sits. A few area pages cover it:

  • Inner suburbs: Mt Victoria, Thorndon, Kelburn, Aro Valley, Newtown.
  • Western and northern suburbs: Karori, Wadestown, Johnsonville, Khandallah.
  • Eastern and southern suburbs: Miramar, Lyall Bay, Island Bay, Seatoun. Salt and southerlies on the coast.
  • Hutt Valley: Lower Hutt, Petone, Upper Hutt, Eastbourne.
  • Porirua and the Kāpiti Coast, if you cover them.

05Where Wellington roofing sites miss

  • Generic photos that could be from anywhere. Wellington's hillside houses are distinctive and build instant trust.
  • No LBP details, even though some roofing work here needs a licensed practitioner.
  • No mention of wind, which is the first thing on a Wellington homeowner's mind.
  • Service areas that ignore the Hutt and Porirua, where a lot of the work is.
  • Old sites that are hard to use on a phone.

06Common questions

Is it worth writing about earthquake-related reroofs?+

Yes, if you do them. Many owners are still considering replacing heavy roofs, and a clear page with examples and a plain explanation helps them decide. Keep the explanation in plain words, and avoid making claims about how a building will perform in a future quake.

Should I list Hutt Valley suburbs separately?+

If you do a good share of work there, give the Hutt its own page with local jobs. Homeowners there often search with Lower Hutt or Upper Hutt in the query.

How do I explain weather delays to customers?+

Put it on the site before anyone asks. Say that you will not work on a roof in high wind, how you let customers know about delays and how you protect the roof if a job is paused part way.

What photos work best for Wellington roofers?+

Ones that show the site: steep sections, scaffold on a hillside, the view across the harbour from the roof. They prove you can handle the access, which many Wellington homeowners worry about.

Should I mention chimney removal?+

If you do it, yes. Plenty of owners still have brick chimneys they want gone, and they search for it as a separate job. Explain what the removal involves and how the roof is made good afterwards.

If you are a Wellington roofer and your site does not reflect the conditions you work in, start a brief. Christchurch roofers have their own earthquake-era story, covered in the Christchurch guide.
