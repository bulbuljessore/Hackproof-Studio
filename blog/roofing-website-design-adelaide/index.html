Roofing website design in Adelaide: stone villas, heatwaves and honest quotes

Adelaide has stone villas with iron roofs in Norwood, post-war tile roofs in the north and new estates in the south. Here is how a roofing site can speak to each of them.

Short answer

In Adelaide, many roofing jobs start with a referral, so the website's job is to confirm it. Lead with the work you do best, whether that is iron roofs on stone villas or tile restoration, show recent local photos, and make it easy to send a picture of the problem with a quote request.

Adelaide is smaller than the eastern capitals, and word of mouth still carries a lot of roofing work. That does not make the website less important. When someone gets your name from a neighbour, the first thing they do is look you up. If the site looks abandoned or generic, the referral loses its weight.

01What roofs do Adelaide homeowners live under?

The older inner suburbs have a lot of sandstone and bluestone villas, many with corrugated iron roofs and bullnose verandahs. You see them across Norwood, Unley, Kensington, Prospect and North Adelaide. Owners of these homes care about keeping the character, and many are in heritage or character zones.

Further out, the post-war and later suburbs are mostly brick with concrete or terracotta tile. The northern suburbs around Salisbury and Elizabeth and the southern ones like Morphett Vale have a lot of tile roofs that are now due for restoration or replacement. Newer estates such as Mawson Lakes, Mount Barker and the southern fringe tend to have Colorbond.

Your website should make it obvious which of these you are best at. If heritage iron roofs are your strength, lead with photos of villas you have re-roofed and explain how you handle the details. If tile restoration is most of your work, lead with that.

02Heat, then storms

Adelaide summers bring long runs of very hot days. Heat ages roof coatings, dries out the mortar on ridge caps and makes thermal movement in metal roofs more noticeable. Then a winter front or a spring storm arrives and the weak points show up as leaks.

The September 2016 storm that caused a statewide blackout is the event many people remember, but smaller wind and hail events happen most years. A storm repair page, written plainly, is worth having even if storm work is a small part of your year.

  • Explain what you check after a storm: lifted sheets, cracked tiles, flashings, gutters.
  • Tell people what to photograph before you arrive.
  • Say how quickly you can usually attend, and be honest about busy periods.

03How should an Adelaide roofer organise area pages?

Adelaide homeowners know their city by area. Area pages built around real jobs work well here:

  • Eastern suburbs: Norwood, Burnside, Magill, Kensington.
  • Inner south: Unley, Goodwood, Mitcham.
  • Western suburbs and the beach: Glenelg, Henley Beach, West Lakes. Salt air matters here.
  • Northern suburbs: Salisbury, Elizabeth, Golden Grove, Mawson Lakes.
  • Southern suburbs and the hills: Morphett Vale, Aldinga, Stirling, Mount Barker.

04Where Adelaide roofing sites fall short

  • No photos of local homes. Adelaide's stone villas are easy to recognise, and real photos of them build trust quickly.
  • No way to send a photo with a quote request. Many roof problems are obvious from one picture.
  • Reviews from years ago and nothing recent. Ask happy customers for a Google review after each job.
  • A homepage that talks about the company before it says what you do and where.
  • Missing licence details, which South Australian homeowners may look for.

The website's job is to confirm what the neighbour said. It needs to show the work and make it easy to get in touch.

05Common questions

My work mostly comes from referrals. Do I still need a proper website?+

Yes. Referred customers often look you up before calling. A clear site with real photos turns that referral into a call. A poor one gives them a reason to keep looking.

Should I show heritage roof work separately?+

If you do it, yes. Owners of stone villas search for heritage roofing specifically, and a page with your own photos is more convincing than any general claim. If you have worked under a heritage overlay or character zone, name it on the page.

Should my website mention heat damage?+

If you see it often, yes. Long hot spells age coatings and dry out ridge cap mortar, and the leaks often show up months later. A short page explaining that link helps people understand what they are paying for.

Can I use photos from my phone on my website?+

Yes. Clear phone photos of real jobs beat stock images every time. Shoot in daylight, keep the whole roof in frame and take the before photo before you start. Ask the crew to shoot the same angle on every job so the gallery looks consistent.

How often should I update my website?+

Add new job photos and reviews every month or two, and check that phone numbers, services and areas are still right. A site that looks cared for is more convincing.
